New website — three design options

Same club information in all three. Only the look and layout change, so we're comparing design, not content.

These are working drafts. Open each one, scroll all the way down, and try it on your phone as well as a computer. Everything is real club content pulled from our current site — fees, training grid, venues, committee contacts. Training times still need confirming after trials, and fees are only published for U8–U12 so far.

Option A

Storm

Dark, bold and cinematic. Big animated logo, lightning motifs, sections that fade in as you scroll.

Feels like
A sports brand or team hype video
Best for
Making a strong first impression on new families
Trade-off
Dark screens are a bit heavier to read, and it leans on animation

Option B

Clubhouse

Light, warm and friendly. Opens by asking "What brings you here?" and points people straight to what they need.

Feels like
A well-run community club that's easy to deal with
Best for
Parents trying to find something quickly on a phone
Trade-off
Calmer, so less visually dramatic than the other two

Option C

Court

Graphic and punchy, built on basketball court markings. Heavy rules and blocks, like a printed poster or a scoresheet.

Feels like
A modern sportswear or athletics brand
Best for
The strongest, most memorable club identity
Trade-off
The boldest of the three, so most likely to divide opinion

What to look for when you review

  • 1
    Could a new parent find the fees? Try it without scrolling the whole page first.
  • 2
    Check it on your phone. Most families will read this in a car park or a gym.
  • 3
    Does it look like us? All three use our logo colours, but they carry different personalities.
  • 4
    Is the training grid clear? That's the page people will open most often during the season.
  • 5
    Would you share it on Facebook? Registration links get posted a lot.
  • 6
    Mix and match is fine. We can take the hero from one and the layout from another.
